# Experiment #58 — luck simulation: zero-skill investors (2026-10-27 17:37 UTC)

Nobody simulated here has any skill. Every 'success' below is luck.

## A Coin-flip gurus (68 gurus × 97 forecasts, each a coin flip)
- Best guru (median over 2,000 simulated worlds): **62% right**; worst: **38%**.
- Share of gurus ≥ 60 % right: 2.1%; ≤ 40 %: 2.1%.
- Chance that at least one guru reaches ≥ 65 %: 7%; at least one ≤ 30 %: 0%.
- Compare CXO Advisory (real gurus 2005–2012): average 47 %. A spread like this is what pure chance produces — a 'star' guru with 65 % is expected even if nobody can forecast.

## B 10,000 skill-less fund managers (fee 1 %, tracking error 6 %)
- Beat the index **5 years in a row** (years 1–5): **169** managers.
- Beat the index **10 years in a row**: **1**.
- A winning streak of ≥ 5 years somewhere in 15 years: 1103; ≥ 8 years: 59.
- 'Statistically proven' skill after 10 years (t > 2): **123** — all pure luck.
- Behind the index after 15 years: **77%** (fees). Top 1 % are ahead by +45% — and would be on magazine covers.

## C Simulated stock market: 10,000 stocks, 20 years, no skill anywhere
Average stock (= equal-weight index) ×3.8; T-bills ×1.8.
- Stocks below T-bills after 20 years: **72%**; lost money: 62%; went bust: 26%.
- The best **10.9%** of stocks create ALL net wealth of the market (Bessembinder: ~4 % for US 1926–2016).
- ×100 stocks: 44; ×1000: 0; best ×680 — produced by luck in a market where nobody can forecast.
